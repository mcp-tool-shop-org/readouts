### Catastrophic forgetting: LoRA as the regularizer, plus the rank/effective-LR/replay levers · `recommended` · ▸ reproduced
**Catastrophic forgetting (the fine-tune is good on-task but loses base capabilities) WORSENS with model scale and with how far the weights move, so the primary fix is to constrain the update -- LoRA forgets less than full fine-tuning AND less than weight-decay/dropout -- backed by lower effective-LR, fewer epochs, and general-instruction replay.**
Luo et al. show catastrophic forgetting is general across 1B-7B LLMs during continual fine-tuning and INTENSIFIES with scale -- so on a 24-34B target the risk is higher, not lower. The mechanism is weight movement: full fine-tuning learns perturbations of rank 10-100x greater than LoRA, and the larger the move, the more the base is overwritten. The load-bearing fix is therefore to CONSTRAIN the update: (1) use LoRA, which 'learns less and forgets less' and -- critically -- mitigates forgetting MORE than common regularizers (weight decay, dropout) while keeping generations diverse; (2) keep the effective rate and epoch count modest (the same over-train that fries a diffusion LoRA over-writes an LLM's base knowledge); (3) mix in general-instruction data / a replay set -- Luo et al. find general instruction tuning alleviates subsequent forgetting. On the diffusion side this is the same phenomenon as DreamBooth 'language drift' (the class is forgotten), cured by the prior-preservation set. The trap: chasing on-task gains by raising rank/LR/epochs directly trades away base capability; you must measure an OUT-OF-DOMAIN retention task, not just the target metric.
- **For the pipeline:** Forgetting is a recipe-field problem (network_type=LoRA over full-FT, modest epochs/effective-LR, a replay/general-instruction mix), and the acceptance bar MUST include an out-of-domain retention eval -- a target-only metric cannot detect forgetting. The validator flags any full-fine-tune of a 24-34B base without a retention gate.

### Frying / saturation: the effective-LR = unet_lr x (alpha/rank) runaway and its cure · `recommended` · ▸ reproduced
**A 'fried' style-LoRA (burnt color, deep-fried contrast, artifacts, prompt non-responsiveness) is almost always an over-large EFFECTIVE learning rate, and the effective rate is unet_lr x (alpha/rank) -- not unet_lr alone -- so raising alpha without lowering lr silently multiplies the rate.**
kohya/sd-scripts scales the LoRA update by alpha/rank (network_alpha / network_dim); the rate the optimizer actually applies is unet_lr x (alpha/rank). The classic 'frying' symptom set -- oversaturated/burnt color, high-contrast deep-fried look, JPEG-like artifacts, and a LoRA that ignores the prompt at weight 1.0 -- is the over-trained/over-rate signature. The three coupled levers are unet_lr, the alpha/rank ratio, and total steps (repeats x epochs x images / batch). Cure order: (1) compute the effective rate and bring it into the style range (start ~5e-5 unet_lr for styles vs ~1e-4 for characters); (2) keep alpha at rank or rank/2 for styles (alpha=rank => scale 1.0, a known-good anchor); (3) if symptoms persist at a sane rate, reduce total steps / pick an earlier epoch checkpoint. Inference-side: a fried LoRA also presents at high apply-weight, so also test at 0.6-0.8 strength before concluding the train is bad. Prodigy/DAdapt hide this because LR=1.0 is a sentinel, not a rate -- there the lever is d_coef/decay + steps, not lr.
- **For the pipeline:** The recipe's unet_lr field is NOT the rate; the planner/validator must surface effective_lr = unet_lr x (alpha/rank) and flag any style recipe whose effective rate exceeds the style band. alpha and rank are a coupled pair, never tuned independently. Ship an 'effective-LR' computed column next to every diffusion recipe.

### Overfit/replication and style-bleed: the caption+pruning+regularization fix, not the LR fix · `recommended` · ▸ reproduced
**Memorization/replication of training images and 'style-bleed' (the trigger drags in unwanted subjects/poses, or the class itself drifts) are DATA failures cured at the caption + pruning + regularization fields -- a prior-preservation set and correct style-vs-subject tag pruning, NOT a lower learning rate.**
Two distinct DATA-side failures often confused with frying. (a) Overfit/replication: the model reproduces training images near-verbatim and loses pose/appearance variability -- the DreamBooth-identified overfitting + 'language drift' (the model forgets how to render the broader class as the trigger overfits). Ruiz et al. cure it with an autogenous class-specific prior-preservation loss: generate class regularization images and train on them alongside the subject. (b) Style-bleed: because the style binds to whatever is UNCAPTIONED, leaving subject/pose/background tags in the captions makes the trigger absorb them, so prompting the trigger drags in a couch or a specific face. The pruning rule is the lever: caption everything you do NOT want the style to own (subjects, poses, backgrounds) and prune the tags describing the style itself, so the residual style signal collapses onto the trigger word. Fix order: tighten captions (booru/wd14 tags keyed to SDXL) + apply the style-vs-subject pruning rule; add a regularization/prior-preservation set sized to the train set; only then touch repeats/epochs. Lowering LR alone does not fix a caption-caused bleed.
- **For the pipeline:** Replication and style-bleed route to dataset fields (caption_strategy, pruning_rule, reg_image_count), not to unet_lr. The validator must NOT recommend 'lower the LR' for a bleed symptom; it must point at the caption/pruning/regularization datasheet fields. Prior-preservation set size and the pruning rule are first-class recipe inputs.
- **Method:** dreambooth · **Applies to:** diffusion · **Base:** SDXL · **Kind:** failure-fix

- **Base model (model-knowledge):** `sdxl-base-1.0`

### Reward-hacking / verbosity-bias and likelihood-displacement in DPO: the beta + data-filtering fix · `recommended` · ▸ reproduced
**Two DPO-specific failures -- (a) responses get longer and more verbose without getting better (length reward-hacking) and (b) the chosen response's probability DROPS during training and can shift mass to an opposite-meaning or unsafe response (likelihood displacement) -- are cured at the DATA (preference-pair) and beta fields, not by more steps.**
DPO inherits human/annotator length bias: models systematically lengthen outputs because longer answers are preferred even at equal quality, so 'reward goes up, quality flat, length up' is reward-hacking, not learning. Worse, Razin et al. show DPO can DECREASE the chosen response's likelihood during training (likelihood displacement) and shift probability mass to a contradictory response -- training to prefer 'No' over 'Never' can sharply raise 'Yes', and aligning Llama-3-8B-Instruct to refuse unsafe prompts dropped its refusal rate from 74.4% to 33.4%. The driver is preference pairs whose chosen/rejected induce SIMILAR embeddings (high CHES score). Fixes: (1) verbosity -- length-disentangle the objective (length-desensitized / length-disentangled DPO variants) or length-balance the pairs, and report a length-controlled win-rate so length gains can't masquerade as quality; (2) displacement -- compute the CHES score over the preference set and FILTER OUT the high-CHES (too-similar) pairs so chosen and rejected are sufficiently distinct; (3) beta is the regularization knob: too-low beta lets the policy drift far from the reference (amplifying both pathologies), so raise beta if displacement/verbosity appear. This is a preference-data + beta problem, not a step-count problem.
- **For the pipeline:** The DPO recipe must carry (a) a length-controlled win-rate as the acceptance metric (a bare win-rate is gameable by length) and (b) a CHES-based preference-pair filter as a dataset preprocessing step. beta is the contrastive-strength regularizer to raise when the policy drifts. The judge for the win-rate MUST be a different model family from the policy (EXTERNAL_VERIFIER).
