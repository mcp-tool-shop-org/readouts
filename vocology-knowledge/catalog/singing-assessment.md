### The effects of feedback interventions on performance: A historical review, a meta-analysis, and a preliminary feedback intervention theory · `load-bearing`
**Meta-analysis of 607 effect sizes over 23,663 observations (domain-general across task types, NOT music-specific -- treat as a design prior, not a singing result): feedback interventions improved performance on average by d = .41, BUT over one third of feedback interventions DECREASED performance, and this could not be explained by sampling error, by feedback sign (praise vs criticism), or by existing theories. The proposed Feedback Intervention Theory attributes it to locus of attention across three hierarchically ordered levels -- task learning, task motivation, and meta-task/self processes -- with the empirical moderator result that feedback effectiveness DECREASES as attention moves up the hierarchy toward the self and away from the task.**
- **Implication:** The single most load-bearing design rule for a practice companion: keep every message pointed at the task ('bar 12 beat 3 sat 40 cents flat and entered 90 ms late') and never at the person ('your intonation is weak', skill grades, leaderboards), because self-directed feedback is the mechanism by which more than a third of feedback interventions make performance worse.
