### CredentialCreate / CredentialAccept / CredentialDelete · `transaction` · ● live
The three-transaction lifecycle of a credential. CredentialCreate (sent by the issuer) mints a credential for a Subject with a CredentialType, optional Expiration, and optional URI. CredentialAccept (sent by the subject) marks it accepted/valid (sets lsfAccepted). CredentialDelete (issuer or subject) removes it, which is also the revocation mechanism.
- **Chain:** xrpl-mainnet · **XLS:** XLS-70 · **Amendment:** Credentials · **Enabled:** 2025 · **Maturity:** stable · **Use:** recommended
- **Key fields / API:** ["Account (issuer)", "Subject", "CredentialType (max 64 bytes)", "Expiration", "URI (max 256 bytes)", "Issuer", "CredentialAccept", "CredentialDelete", "lsfAccepted"]
- **Gotchas:** ["Issuer and Subject may be the same account (self-issued credential) — relying parties should verify the Issuer is a trusted authority, not just that a credential exists.", "CredentialType is hex on the wire and capped at 64 bytes; URI is capped at 256 bytes — keep payloads tiny and push real data off-ledger.", "Until CredentialAccept runs, the credential exists but is not usable for DepositPreauth or domain membership.", "CredentialDelete by the subject is a legitimate self-removal path; issuers cannot prevent a subject from deleting an accepted credential."]
- **Build-focus tracks:** [identity-compliance](track-identity-compliance.md)
- **Verify:** cross-family(deepseek-v3.1:671b)=unverified | net-check: depends on XLS-70 which is unverified | currency=unknown | Transaction types require XLS-70 Credentials amendment, whose mainnet status is unconfirmed.
- **Sources:** [CredentialCreate — xrpl.org transaction reference](https://xrpl.org/docs/references/protocol/transactions/types/credentialcreate) — CredentialCreate fields are Account (issuer), Subject, CredentialType (max 64 bytes), optional Expiration, and optional URI (max 256 bytes); issuer and subject may be the same account.

### Credentials (on-chain attestations) · `amendment` · ● live
A W3C-aligned framework for issuing signed, on-ledger attestations about an account (KYC status, accreditation, region eligibility). An issuer mints a Credential to a subject; the subject must accept it for it to become valid. Credentials are the building block that powers credential-based Deposit Authorization, Permissioned Domains, and Permissioned DEXes. It stores only an issuer attestation plus an optional URI — never the underlying PII.
- **Chain:** xrpl-mainnet · **XLS:** XLS-70 · **Amendment:** Credentials · **Enabled:** 2025 · **Maturity:** stable · **Use:** recommended
- **Key fields / API:** ["Credential (ledger entry)", "Issuer", "Subject", "CredentialType", "Expiration", "URI", "lsfAccepted flag"]
- **Gotchas:** ["A credential is inert until the subject accepts it (CredentialAccept sets lsfAccepted); relying parties must check the accepted flag, not just existence.", "Credentials carry no PII — they only attest 'issuer X says subject Y has type Z'; trust is entirely in the off-chain issuer/verifier, so region-gating is only as good as the issuer.", "Expiration is enforced at use time: an offer, payment, or domain membership silently becomes invalid once the credential expires.", "Revocation is by deletion (CredentialDelete) — there is no on-chain revocation history, so auditing past validity requires off-ledger logging.", "CredentialType is opaque arbitrary bytes (max 64); both issuer and relying party must agree on its meaning out-of-band."]

### Decentralized Identifiers (DID) · `amendment` · ● live ✓
Adds a W3C-conformant Decentralized Identifier (the did:xrpl method) anchored to an XRPL account. The owner controls a DID ledger entry holding an on-ledger minimal DID document, arbitrary Data, or a URI pointing to an off-ledger document (IPFS/STORJ). It is the self-sovereign identity root that Verifiable Credentials and compliance flows reference. Control of the DID equals control of the account's private key.
- **Chain:** xrpl-mainnet · **XLS:** XLS-40 · **Amendment:** DID · **Enabled:** 2024-10-31 · **Maturity:** stable · **Use:** recommended
- **Key fields / API:** ["DIDSet (transaction)", "DIDDelete (transaction)", "DID (ledger entry)", "DIDDocument", "Data", "URI", "did:xrpl method"]
- **Gotchas:** ["DIDs are fully public — never store PII, KYC documents, or personal data on-ledger; reference content-addressed off-ledger storage (IPFS) instead.", "One DID per account (the DID ledger entry is keyed to the account); there is no multi-DID-per-account model.", "Implicit DID documents can be derived from the account's public key even with no DIDDocument/URI set, which surprises builders expecting an empty resolve.", "DID alone proves no identity — it must be paired with Credentials/Verifiable Credentials from a trusted issuer to mean anything for KYC."]

### Deposit Authorization (DepositAuth flag) · `concept` · ● live ✓
An account flag (asfDepositAuth, value 9 / lsfDepositAuth 0x01000000) that blocks all incoming transfers of XRP and tokens from strangers. Once set, the account can only receive funds from explicitly preauthorized senders, via two-step protocols (Checks, Escrow, Payment Channels), or by self-initiated transactions. It is the foundational on/off switch for a compliant, allow-list-only account.
- **Chain:** xrpl-mainnet · **Amendment:** DepositAuth · **Enabled:** 2018 · **Maturity:** stable · **Use:** recommended
- **Key fields / API:** ["asfDepositAuth (9)", "lsfDepositAuth (0x01000000)", "AccountSet SetFlag/ClearFlag", "Checks / Escrow / Payment Channels (two-step receipt)"]
- **Gotchas:** ["Even with DepositAuth on, an account below its reserve can still receive XRP up to the reserve amount from anyone — not a hard block at low balances.", "Rippling and DEX offers create exceptions, so DepositAuth does not stop all token inflows; pair with RequireAuth/freezes for issued assets.", "Preauthorization is currency-agnostic — you cannot preauthorize a sender for one currency only.", "Two-step protocols (Check/Escrow/PayChan) bypass the stranger block because the destination initiates receipt; account for that in compliance assumptions."]
- **Build-focus tracks:** [identity-compliance](track-identity-compliance.md), [payments](track-payments.md)
- **Verify:** cross-family(deepseek-v3.1:671b)=confirmed | net-check: mainnet-live (long-standing feature) | currency=current | DepositAuth flag (asfDepositAuth) is a well-established mainnet feature for account protection.
- **Sources:** [Deposit Authorization — xrpl.org](https://xrpl.org/docs/concepts/accounts/depositauth) — DepositAuth (asfDepositAuth value 9) blocks all transfers from strangers and only allows preauthorized senders, two-step protocols, or self-initiated transactions; preauthorization is currency-agnostic.

### DepositPreauth (account + credential preauthorization) · `transaction` · ● live
Grants exceptions to a DepositAuth-enabled account. The transaction takes exactly one of four fields: Authorize / Unauthorize (by specific account address) or AuthorizeCredentials / UnauthorizeCredentials (by a set of {Issuer, CredentialType} pairs, enabled by the Credentials amendment). Credential-based preauth lets any sender holding the right accepted credentials deposit — no per-account allow-listing — which is the scalable KYC gate.
- **Chain:** xrpl-mainnet · **XLS:** XLS-70 · **Amendment:** Credentials (credential-based fields); DepositPreauth (account-based) · **Enabled:** 2025 · **Maturity:** stable · **Use:** recommended
- **Key fields / API:** ["Authorize", "Unauthorize", "AuthorizeCredentials", "UnauthorizeCredentials", "Issuer", "CredentialType", "DepositPreauth (ledger entry)"]
- **Gotchas:** ["Exactly one of the four fields must be present — mixing account and credential authorization in one transaction fails.", "Each account-based preauth creates a DepositPreauth ledger object that consumes owner reserve; credential-based preauth scales better because one rule covers all matching holders.", "AuthorizeCredentials requires the sender to hold an ACCEPTED credential at deposit time; an unaccepted or expired credential silently fails the deposit.", "Cannot preauthorize yourself; preauthorizations are one-directional."]
- **Build-focus tracks:** [identity-compliance](track-identity-compliance.md), [payments](track-payments.md)
- **Verify:** cross-family(deepseek-v3.1:671b)=unverified | net-check: partially live (account-based), credential features depend on unverified XLS-70 | currency=partial | fix: Account-based DepositPreauth is live; credential fields require XLS-70 Credentials amendment | Traditional DepositPreauth exists, but credential-based authorization requires unverified XLS-70.
- **Sources:** [DepositPreauth — xrpl.org transaction reference](https://xrpl.org/docs/references/protocol/transactions/types/depositpreauth) — DepositPreauth takes exactly one of Authorize, Unauthorize, AuthorizeCredentials, or UnauthorizeCredentials; the credential fields take {Issuer, CredentialType} pairs and are enabled by the Credentials amendment.

### RequireAuth (authorized trust lines) · `concept` · ● live ✓
An issuer account flag (asfRequireAuth, value 2) that requires the issuer to explicitly authorize each trust line before a holder can receive the issuer's token. It is the classic permissioned-issuance gate: combined with KYC off-chain, an issuer only authorizes trust lines for verified accounts, making the token itself allow-list-only. Must be set before the issuer has any tokens outstanding.
- **Chain:** xrpl-mainnet · **Enabled:** n/a · **Maturity:** stable · **Use:** recommended
- **Key fields / API:** ["asfRequireAuth (2)", "lsfRequireAuth", "TrustSet (tfSetfAuth)", "lsfLowAuth / lsfHighAuth"]
- **Gotchas:** ["RequireAuth can only be enabled while the issuer has zero issued balances/trust lines — you cannot retrofit it onto a live token.", "Authorization is per-trust-line and one-way (issuer authorizes holder); it does not gate the holder from sending to third parties once authorized.", "It controls who may HOLD the token, which is a different axis from DepositAuth (who may PAY an account) — regulated assets usually need both plus freezes.", "Manual authorization does not scale; for large user bases prefer Credential-gated Permissioned Domains over per-line authorization."]

### Permissioned Domains · `amendment` · ● live
A managed, rule-based environment within the public ledger where activity is restricted to accounts holding accepted credentials. A domain owner defines AcceptedCredentials (a set of {Issuer, CredentialType}); any account holding a matching accepted credential is a member. Domains are the enforcement envelope that lets banks, exchanges, and regulated games run KYC/AML-gated activity on mainnet, and they are the substrate for Permissioned DEXes, Lending, and Vaults. Went live 4 Feb 2026 with ~91% validator support.

### DID amendment (XLS-40) · `standard` · ● live ✓
The protocol amendment that adds native W3C-conformant Decentralized Identifiers to XRPL. It introduces the DID ledger object plus the DIDSet and DIDDelete transactions, letting any account anchor a self-sovereign identity on-ledger. This is the foundation a studio builds portable cross-game player identity on, so as the enabling standard it rates a 5.
- **Chain:** xrpl-mainnet · **XLS:** XLS-40 (XLS-40d) · **Amendment:** DID · **Enabled:** 2024-10-30 · **Maturity:** production · **Use:** recommended
- **Key fields / API:** Amendment name: DID; amendment ID DB432C3A09D9D5DFC7859F39AE5FF767ABC59AED0A9FB441E83B814D8946C109; shipped in rippled 2.0.0 (2024-01-09); enabled on Mainnet 2024-10-30; supporting amendments fixEmptyDID and fixPreviousTxnID activated in the same window; one DID object per account.
- **Gotchas:** Do not confuse XLS-40 (DIDs) with on-ledger Verifiable Credentials, which are a separate later standard (XLS-70 / Credentials) — XLS-40 gives you the identifier, not an issued-credential registry. The amendment needed a fix cycle (fixEmptyDID) before its Mainnet enablement, so the 'introduced 2.0.0' date (Jan 2024) and the 'enabled' date (Oct 2024) are different; cite the Oct 2024 enablement as the live date.

### DID ledger object · `concept` · ● live ✓
The on-ledger entry that holds (or references) the data for an account's Decentralized Identifier. It is deterministically keyed from the controlling account, so there is exactly one per account and its existence/contents are publicly queryable. Understanding its structure and reserve cost is essential to designing identity storage, so it rates a 4.
- **Chain:** xrpl-mainnet · **XLS:** XLS-40 · **Amendment:** DID · **Enabled:** 2024-10-30 · **Maturity:** production · **Use:** recommended
- **Key fields / API:** Account (AccountID, controller); DIDDocument / Data / URI (each Blob, <=256 bytes, optional); OwnerNode (UInt64, owner-directory hint); PreviousTxnID (UInt256); PreviousTxnLgrSeq (UInt32). Object ID = SHA-512Half(0x0049 + AccountID). Costs one owner reserve to create; reclaimed on DIDDelete. Queryable via the ledger_entry / account_objects APIs.
- **Gotchas:** One DID per account is a hard constraint of the keying scheme — a player who wants multiple personas needs multiple XRPL accounts (each with its own reserve), which has cost and UX implications for a studio. Everything in the object is public and immutable; the 256-byte caps force any real DID document off-ledger. The owner reserve is a live network setting (~2 XRP at mid-2026), not a protocol constant — confirm the current value rather than hardcoding it.

### CredentialAccept transaction · `transaction` · ● live ✓
The subject side of the attestation handshake. A credential is unusable for any gating until accepted, so for a studio this is the explicit player-consent step: the player must opt in to carrying the verified-status credential on their account.
- **Chain:** xrpl-mainnet · **XLS:** XLS-70 · **Amendment:** Credentials · **Enabled:** 2025-09-04 · **Maturity:** production · **Use:** verified
- **Key fields / API:** Account (the subject, required -- ONLY the subject can accept). Issuer (Address, required) -- the account that created the credential. ### Issuer-to-subject attestation model · `concept` · ● live ✓
The conceptual core of XLS-70: a three-role trust model. An AUTHORIZER (e.g. the studio or a regulated platform) decides which issuers it trusts; an ISSUER (a KYC/identity provider) attests typed facts about accounts; and a SUBJECT (the player) holds the resulting credential on their own account and presents it to gate access. The key design choice is that the subject must accept and then owns the credential (passport model), so attestations are portable, consent-based, and reusable across any app that trusts the same issuer+type -- a player who is KYC-verified once can be recognized by many games/services without re-verifying.
- **Chain:** xrpl-mainnet · **XLS:** XLS-70 · **Amendment:** Credentials · **Enabled:** 2025-09-04 · **Maturity:** production · **Use:** verified

Trust is expressed off-chain (which issuers an authorizer accepts) plus on-chain (the accepted-credentials lists). CredentialType is the typed claim; URI optionally links a richer off-chain Verifiable Credential.
- **Gotchas:** There is no global registry of trusted issuers -- each authorizer/app independently decides which issuer accounts + credential types it honors, so a credential is only as good as the verifier's trust list. Issuer-account compromise is a single point of failure (a bad issuer can mint false attestations); rotate issuer keys and scope issuers narrowly. Portability is a feature AND a risk: any app trusting the same issuer+type recognizes the credential, so issuers must scope credential types carefully (e.g. 'over18' is broadly reusable, 'gameX_vip' is not).

### On-chain credential privacy (PII off-ledger) · `concept` · ● live ✓

### On-chain enforcement vs custodial backend: the compliance split decision · `concept` · · n/a ✓
The decisive architecture call for a compliant game economy: the ledger is an ENFORCEMENT layer, not a compliance program. On-chain belongs only what must be verifiable and enforceable by anyone — who may hold/receive/trade value (Credentials, Permissioned Domains, DepositPreauth) and the ability to immobilize/recover it (freeze, clawback). Everything that constitutes the actual KYC/AML program — collecting identity documents, sanctions screening, the audit trail, suspicious-activity reporting, re-verification scheduling, right-to-erasure handling — lives in the custodial backend, which acts as the credential ISSUER and the system of record. Get this split wrong and you either leak PII on an immutable ledger or build unenforceable gates.
- **Chain:** xrpl-mainnet · **Maturity:** evergreen · **Use:** live
- **Key fields / API:** On-chain (enforcement): Credential objects, PermissionedDomain membership, DepositPreauth gates, freeze flags, clawback capability, MPT allow-lists. Off-chain (compliance program): identity dossier, sanctions/PEP screening engine, KYC vendor integration, audit log, SAR workflow, re-KYC scheduler, credential issuance/revocation policy, erasure handling. The bridge: your backend is the credential issuer — it decides when to CredentialCreate (grant) and when to CredentialDelete / let-expire (revoke), turning a backend compliance verdict into an on-chain enforceable state.
- **Gotchas:** Revocation is the hard seam: deleting/expiring a credential stops FUTURE value flows but does not retroactively unwind value already received — for that you need freeze+clawback on the token, so revocation policy and recall policy must be designed together. The ledger has no concept of 'reason' — a frozen or clawed-back state on-chain is just a flag; the legal justification and audit record must live in the backend or you cannot defend the action. Do not treat on-chain credentials as your source of truth for identity — they are derived assertions; the backend dossier is canonical, and the chain is a projection of its current verdict.

### Privacy tradeoffs of on-chain identity (what leaks) · `concept` · · n/a ✓
XRPL Credentials are deliberately PII-minimal: the ledger holds only Issuer, Subject (wallet), CredentialType, Expiration, and an off-chain URI — never the dossier itself. But that minimalism is not full privacy. The mere existence of a credential publicly links a wallet to 'this issuer KYC'd this account,' the CredentialType strings reveal what category of claim was made (age, region, accreditation), and because XRPL is fully transparent, a wallet's entire payment graph is then linkable to that attested identity bucket. Putting any identity-adjacent state on-chain trades surveillance-resistance for verifiability; you must consciously decide what that link reveals.
- **Chain:** xrpl-mainnet · **Maturity:** evergreen · **Use:** live
- **Key fields / API:** On-chain: Issuer AccountID, Subject AccountID, CredentialType string, Expiration, URI; Off-chain: the actual identity document / dossier the URI points to. Leakage vectors: issuer-subject linkage, credential-type semantics, full transaction-graph correlation of an attested wallet, timing/amount analysis. Mitigation directions: generic CredentialType naming, per-feature wallet segregation, zero-knowledge 'over-18' style proofs (gold standard but not native to XLS-70), keeping all real PII strictly off-ledger.
- **Gotchas:** Do NOT put anything resembling PII in the CredentialType or URI that resolves to public data — GDPR-style 'right to erasure' is incompatible with an immutable ledger, so on-chain you keep only cryptographic/categorical references. A single attested wallet that also receives payouts and trades publicly is fully deanonymizable to its identity bucket by graph analysis; if a player uses one wallet for everything, KYC'ing it exposes their whole on-chain life. XLS-70 does not provide zero-knowledge selective disclosure — proving 'over 18' on-chain currently means holding a credential that says so, which is more revealing than a true ZK proof; treat ZK as a backend/off-chain concern for now.

- **Key fields / API:** AccountSet: SetFlag asfDepositAuth(9), asfRequireDest(1), asfDisallowIncomingCheck(13)/PayChan(14)/NFTokenOffer(12)/Trustline(15); DepositPreauth AuthorizeCredentials [{Issuer, CredentialType}]; issuer account AccountSet asfRequireAuth(2) + TrustSet tfSetfAuth; senders attach Payment.CredentialIDs + DestinationTag.
- **Gotchas:** Order matters: enable asfRequireAuth on the issuer BEFORE issuing (needs zero trust lines/Offers). Don't set DisallowIncomingTrustline on a token issuer that needs inbound trust lines — apply that flag only to the pure treasury, not the issuer. Credential-based preauth needs both DepositPreauth + Credentials amendments and the player must CredentialAccept; revoke via CredentialDelete / UnauthorizeCredentials. Keep the issuer fully trusted and its keys protected (compromise = minted deposit access / token holding). Leave the ~1 XRP sub-reserve receive exemption in mind so the treasury can never brick itself. Separate the deposit-receiving treasury from the token-issuer account.
