### training-knowledge: judges, juries and planted pairs (wave 20)

How should the studio build the models that judge other models' answers? This wave brings two of the bench's design
notes in, with only their verified claims.

**Measured on the studio's rigs:**
- **The hardest planted pairs are real errors.** The fine-tuned Kev-4B judge misses 2 or 3 of 149 planted pairs per
  seed, averaged over both orders, and the hardest of them are genuine errors, such as "must be" changed to "is
  usually". The studio's planting pipeline checks for truncation, length and similarity, but never that a plant is
  actually an error. So check a plant before blaming the judge for missing it.
- **On natural errors, pair two judges.** On 95 answers from llama3.1:8b with labelled errors, gemma4:31b alone caught
  0.97 of the flawed answers, at precision 0.72. With muse-glimmer, a judge from another family, the two caught 93% of
  the error sentences. The labels are Claude's: one Opus pass and four blind Sonnet passes,
  with Opus adjudicating; no person has checked them.

**From the literature** (each the paper's own result, read from its abstract and tables, not reproduced):
- judges detect natural LLM errors at low recall, and majority voting doesn't fix it (Kamoi et al. 2024);
- giving a judge a persona didn't improve its factual accuracy, across 162 personas (Zheng et al. 2023);
- training on both answer orders raises a judge's order consistency, from 73.45% to 78.89% in JudgeLM;
- models that both err pick the same wrong answer about 60% of the time on HELM (Kim et al. 2025);
- a small panel of judges (PoLL) leads single judges on two of three QA sets, and trails Claude Haiku on HotpotQA
  (Verga et al. 2024).

**Corrections on the way in:** three figures were fixed against their sources, and one dataset's licence (FELM,
CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) is recorded as internal evaluation only.

### vocology-knowledge: a voice gate, and a syllable dated too late (wave 15)

Two checks from the studio's work on sung vocals, three hymns sung by one synthetic voice.
- **A voice gate.** It uses pyannote's speech segmentation to check that a placed vocal sings its notes and stays quiet
  in its rests. On a published Battle Hymn vocal, the voice level is 0.984 in sung notes and 0.09 in rests.
  - **It's precise, but it misses most short noises.** Against marks made by ear on nine takes, it caught 3 of 20 short
    "honks". All three of its voice-in-rest flags were on marked honks, and the four takes with no marked noise drew
    none.
  - So it's a cheap check beside listening, not a replacement for it.
- **A syllable dated too late.** The aligner dated one syllable 0.7 s late, on its own fading tail, and it came out
  nearly silent, at −42 dB. The fix rejects a date more than 0.3 s from its neighbours' median, and picks that syllable
  from another take; it then sat at −22 dB.
  - The 0.7 s, the −42 dB and the re-pick counts come from the fix's own commit record, because the before-and-after
    plans weren't committed.
  - The lesson: one syllable far from its neighbours is a misdate; a whole take running late is the take.
- **Corrections on the way in:** the voice levels now come from the published receipt, and the tempo range the
  thresholds were set on is 72–92 bpm, not 72–95.

### tensor-engine-knowledge: NVIDIA PAIR and CUDA Tile C++ (wave 21)

Four rows, all from NVIDIA's own documentation. PAIR hasn't been installed on the rig, so none of this is measured here.
- **PAIR 1.0.0** is a router for a home or office network. It sits in front of Ollama, LM Studio and llama.cpp, under
  Apache-2.0.
  - **It routes by queue, not by fit.** Its scheduler weighs pending jobs and a coarse reading of GPU pressure. VRAM,
    latency and which machine already holds the model aren't inputs. So it won't prefer the faster machine, or the
    one with the model loaded.
  - **Its node telemetry is plaintext,** readable by anything on the same subnet, by NVIDIA's own statement. "Local
    first" doesn't mean nothing leaves the machine. This replaces the bench's earlier, unverified "no telemetry beyond
    paired nodes".
- **CUDA Tile C++** is in the CUDA toolkit from 13.3. Tile dimensions are powers of two, fixed when the kernel is
  compiled.

Not loaded: a claim that CUDA 13.4 adds programmatic dependent launch for tile kernels (NVIDIA's pages don't say so),
and PAIR's community-contributed Anthropic Messages support, which wasn't read at its source.

### model-knowledge: where TranslateGemma slips on READMEs (wave 22)

The studio translates every release README on the rig with TranslateGemma 27B, so its slips reach public pages. It's
reliable on prose, and it slipped on two things readers lean on:
- **Counts written "N of M".** In Chinese, "218 of 224" came back reversed, as 224 of 218, and "285 of 414" came back as
  an ordinal, "the 285th of 414". Both were fixed by hand.
- **Product and model names.** In Hindi, "Kimi-K3" was spelled out in Devanagari. It was restored by hand, came back
  from the translation cache on the next run, and was fixed again.

The check that follows: compare lines that should stay verbatim before and after, read every "N of M" cell, and clear a
hand-fixed line from the cache so the fix holds.

The limit: one README, two languages, two runs. It's a pattern to check for, not an error rate. The model is under the
Gemma licence, gated on Hugging Face.
