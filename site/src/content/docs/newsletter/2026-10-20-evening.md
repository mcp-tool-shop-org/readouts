## Promoted from the research bench

The promotion queue moved again this evening. Each of these came from
[rnd](https://github.com/mcp-tool-shop-org/rnd), and each loads only what was re-read against its receipts.

### training-knowledge: what more prompts fix, and what they don't (wave 19)

This continues this afternoon's wave 18 on the ASPIRE critic, a small model trained to tell a strong answer from a
flawed one.
- **More prompts raise the level, not the spread.** At 128 training prompts instead of 32, every seed improved by 0.10
  to 0.13, and the mean rose from 0.635 to 0.745 on 127 planted-error pairs. The gap between the best and worst seed
  stayed 0.181 at both prompt counts.
- **A critic can learn backwards.** One critic init scored 0.315, its whole interval below 0.5: it had learned to
  prefer the flawed answer. Critic init alone spanned 0.488, the run seed 0.283, against a noise floor of 0.055. The
  0.488 rests on that one draw; the other two inits differ by only 0.016.
- **The backwards critic holds up.** On 149 fresh pairs it had never seen, it still preferred the flawed answer 0.678
  of the time. Read as an auditor that flags flaws, that's the bar a deliberately trained one has to beat.
- **What to do:** train several inits, select on a held-out set (never the judge set), and reject any critic below 0.5.
  The limit: one student size and three seeds per arm, so the ranges are rough.

### model-knowledge: EmbeddingGemma 2, measured on the studio's own corpus (wave 20)

EmbeddingGemma 2 is Google DeepMind's open embedding model: text, code, images, video and audio in one 768-dimension
space. It's Apache 2.0, and its card asks deployments to follow the Gemma Prohibited Use Policy. The studio's tools use
it: offrig adopts it, and the dataset desk ships it as a backend.
- **On the studio's pre-registered benchmark** (93 queries over offrig's corpus), it beat nomic-embed-text by +0.112
  dense nDCG@10, with a one-sided 95% lower bound of +0.065. Code gained most, +0.174; prose +0.041.
- **Cutting it to 256 dimensions** lost 0.043 and failed its bar.
- **Never run it in float16:** its card says the activations overflow, giving NaN or quietly degraded vectors. Use
  bfloat16 or float32.
- The benchmark decides for offrig on one rig; it isn't a general ranking. Google's MTEB figures are recorded as the
  card states them.

### tensor-engine-knowledge: CUDA Python on consumer Blackwell (wave 20)

NVIDIA's CUDA Python libraries don't all say whether they run on the RTX 5090's compute capability 12.0. A smoke rung
checked: each library ran one small kernel, checked against NumPy.
- **All six ran correctly:** cuda.core, CuPy, numba-cuda, cuda.compute, nvmath-python and cuTile. So cuTile does run on
  consumer Blackwell.
- **Hold NumPy below 2.5:** numba-cuda 0.30.4, with numba 0.68.0, fails on NumPy 2.5, which removed a function numba
  still calls.
- **CuPy carries its own runtime:** CuPy 14.2.0 uses its built-in CUDA 13.2 runtime, while NVRTC and cuBLASLt come
  from the CUDA 13 wheels.
- **The licences:** the libraries are Apache-2.0, MIT and BSD-2-Clause. The CUDA runtime wheels they pull in carry
  NVIDIA's [CUDA EULA](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/eula/index.html).
- A smoke rung shows each library installs and runs here; it says nothing about speed. One rig, one driver, one Python
  version.

### model-knowledge: open Jev-style decision models, and the studio's Kev runs (wave 21)

Jev-style decision models answer small, typed decisions quickly. This wave records three open ones, with the
projects' own figures labelled as theirs, and the studio's own runs of Kev.
- **Kev** is an Apache-2.0 family; its project reports scores close to Jev's on its own benchmark.
  - **As a forecaster** on the studio's sense-si records, Kev-4B and Kev-9B did no better than the base rate: Brier
    0.240 and 0.242 against 0.242, AUC 0.54 and 0.52, on 124 records.
  - **As a judge** of planted errors, Kev-4B is badly position-biased as a single choice: 0.547, choosing A 95% of the
    time. Averaged over both answer orders, it scored 0.973 on 149 fresh pairs, ahead of Qwen2.5-32B's 0.886.
  - **A 22-minute fine-tune** removed the position bias on three of three seeds, and held at 0.989 on another model's
    planted errors.
- **Laya**, 421M parameters, Apache-2.0: its authors report 0.766 for the fine-tuned checkpoint on their own
  typed-decisions benchmark, against 0.362 for the base model. There's no committed result file for that yet.
- **Bespoke-Nimble-9B** agrees with its benchmark's reference labels 90.12% of the time; Jev scores 93.21% on the same
  labels. That's agreement with the labels, not with Jev.
- **Three corrections on the way in:** a timing first described as Apple Silicon was measured on an AMD GPU in an Intel
  Mac Pro; Nimble's "90% of Jev's answers" became agreement with reference labels; and Kev's request sizes became 1,562
  to 4,721 tokens, median 3,045.
